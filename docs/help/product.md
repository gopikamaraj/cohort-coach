# Cohort Coach: how to use the app

## What the app does

Cohort Coach helps a Grade 3 teacher find the one mistake many of her pupils make, and then helps her fix it. A short check of ten questions shows what children get wrong. Teachers whose classes make the same mistake follow the same three-week track on that one mistake. Before she tries a new way of teaching on real children, she can rehearse it with five simulated children in the Practice Class. Everything in the demo uses simulated data.

## The screens

### Home

Home says how many children you checked this term and what your class's focus is. One button takes you to the next step. The other tiles open Practice, My programme, What I entered, Watch a rehearsal and My profile.

### Enter check

You enter what each child wrote on the ten-question check. You can photograph the answer sheets in roll order, or type the answers. The phone shows which boxes it could not read and asks you to confirm them. A sheet photo is read once and not kept, and no child's name is ever entered.

### My class

My class shows one card for each mistake that children in your class make. Each card says how many of the children you checked make that mistake, and shows an example from your own class with a drawing of sticks. The card for your class's focus says so. You can tap the speaker to hear a card read aloud, and tap the flag if a card looks wrong.

### Practice

Practice is a rehearsal with five simulated children who make your own class's mistakes. You type or speak what you would say to them, and tap materials such as bundles of ten or loose sticks. When you finish, you get private feedback with two things that went well and one thing to try, each quoting your own words. It never gives a score. The children are made up, and the rehearsal stays on your phone unless you choose to share it.

### My programme

My programme shows where you are in your three-week track, step by step, and which lessons come next. Each lesson is about five minutes and arrives as a voice note. After a lesson you are asked two quick questions: whether you tried it in class, and how it went.

### What I entered

What I entered lists the checks you have entered, with each child's answers on request and whether the results have been sent.

### My profile

My profile holds your language, your classes, how much storage and data the app uses, backup and restore, your consent choices, the privacy notice and a feedback form. You can change language here, and you can erase everything.

## The mistakes the app looks for

- Writes a number the way it is said: forty-three becomes 403.
- Drops the carried ten: 47 plus 25 becomes 62.
- Takes the smaller digit from the larger in each column: 52 minus 29 becomes 37.
- Borrows a ten but does not reduce the tens digit: 52 minus 29 becomes 33.
- Swaps tens and ones: 43 becomes 34. The app notices this one but has no track for it yet.

## Working without signal

Most of the app works with no signal. Entering a check, opening saved lessons and reading your cards all work offline, and results are sent when the phone finds signal again. The Practice Class needs a connection, and when the signal is weak it falls back to typing.

## Keeping the app on your phone

You can save the app on your phone's home screen like any other app. On the first screen, tap How to install and follow the steps. Saving it is optional: it also works in the browser.

## Your privacy

No child's name, face or voice is collected. Per-child answers stay on your phone; only counts for your whole class are sent, and only if you agree. The assistant never sees a child's name, and it cannot see other teachers' data.

## What the assistant can and cannot do

The assistant answers questions about how to use the app and what its screens mean, using only these help pages. It cites the page it used and tells you how to check. It cannot approve, send or delete anything, it cannot see other teachers, and it will say so when it does not know.

## Does the app use AI, and how does the assistant find its answers?

Yes, in a few places, and always with checks. In the Practice Class an AI plays the pretend children and labels the move you made, and after you finish it gives feedback that quotes your own words. Every number about a class is worked out by the app's own code, never by the AI.

The assistant uses retrieval-augmented generation, often called RAG. It first searches these help pages for passages about your question, matching both the words and the meaning. Then a small AI model writes a short answer using only those passages, and the app checks that every quote really is on the page before it shows you the answer. If the pages do not cover your question, the assistant says so instead of guessing, and each answer lists the page it came from.
