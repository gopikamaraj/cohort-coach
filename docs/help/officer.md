# Programme officer help: block, DIET and SCERT

## How do I read this verdict

A verdict compares the fall in children's mistake rate in the classes that followed a track with the fall in a comparison group, per track version. At block scale the effect is the track group's fall minus the comparison group's fall, with a 95 percent interval from resampling classes. A track is Working when the whole interval is below zero and the fall is at least ten points. It is Not working when the fall is under five points or the rate rose. Anything else is Inconclusive, and so is any result with fewer than fifteen classes in an arm. A failed track counts against the track, never against a teacher.

## Why can't I see names

The programme view shows aggregates only. You cannot see a teacher's class or a child, and any figure covering fewer than five classes is hidden and says so. This protects teachers: the system is built so that nobody is judged by a number about her own class.

## What does this chart not show

A chart of class counts does not show why a class changed, only that it did, compared with the comparison group. It does not show anything for groups under five classes. Small groups are read as directional and carry a confidence note such as "from 3 classes". In the demo, every chart is simulated data and is labelled as such. A chart of modules delivered shows what was sent, not whether anyone listened or taught differently.

## How were cohorts formed

Teachers whose classes share a mistake are grouped across schools for the track on that mistake, not by geography. A class qualifies when at least a quarter of the children checked show the mistake and at least ten children were checked. Each class follows one track a term. Eligible teachers for a track are randomly split between this term and next, stratified by their starting rate, so next term's teachers form the comparison group. A cohort has fifteen to twenty teachers, at most two from one school, and one mentor.

## Is this real data

In the demo, no. Every chart, cohort and verdict in the demo comes from a simulated block of made-up classes, and each screen says "simulated". The pilot server is real but holds no real class results yet. The method for real results is built and tested: the verdict rules and the pilot's paired test.

## The funnel and where its numbers come from

The funnel shows four steps for each cohort and track version. Delivered counts modules sent, from the system. Tried comes from teachers' own taps, so it is self-report. Practised counts Practice Class sessions, from the system. Changed comes only from the next check. Until then it reads "awaiting next check". Delivered and Practised show engagement, not adoption.

## What the pilot adds

At pilot scale the method is different: the fall in the trained mistake is compared with the average fall of mistakes that were not trained, and the paired change in children is tested with an exact McNemar test. The method is built and tested; no real class has been through it yet.

## Approving cohorts and content

You approve proposed cohorts and, with your key, switch approved content on after the coach has approved it. You cannot approve content yourself: the coach does that. The assistant cannot approve anything.
