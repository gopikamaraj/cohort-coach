**Challenge 3 evidence digest: teacher support and training**

Sources (PDF pages): **BaSE** BaSE Report 2025 (CSF teacher survey) · **FLN** CSF, Systemic Drivers of FLN · **AIC** AI Impact Summit 2026 casebook · **NITI** NITI Aayog report, 2026 · **IDR** IDR article, 2025 · **APU** Azim Premji University article, 2025. Rows: statistics, observations, then opinion. (?) = uncertain.

## G1. No continuous, practical guidance after training

| Evidence | Sources |
|---|---|
| Head teachers fully satisfied with support: BRC 42%, CRC 52.6%; feedback 66% procedural, 26% pedagogical (one State); BRC/CRC admin time 54%/50% | FLN 58 |
| Trained on appointment: CRC 54%, BRC 30%, BEO 32%, DEO 14%; 71% of DEOs, 42% of BEOs double-charged; 50–60% block/district and over 50% SCERT/DIET posts vacant in places | FLN 59, 110; NITI 145, 147 |
| Under a third of teachers trained yearly, and falling | FLN 54 |
| No tech help: 17% can't fix problems; 18% of non-users know no user; 20% unfamiliar with school devices; 68% of GenAI non-users lack know-how; 18% ask peers (?) | BaSE 53, 55, 59, 63, 66 |
| Teachers find tools themselves (37%), via peers (36%) or admin (36%) | BaSE 57 |
| Visits check registers; feedback vague and late; CRPs act as "post masters" | FLN 58, 72, 74; NITI 145, 177 |
| Teachers seldom act on junior, contract CRCs' advice | FLN 59 |
| Classroom feedback rare; rural and new teachers lack mentoring; contract teachers miss development | AIC 74; NITI 147, 148 |
| No sustained AI mentoring; faculty development rarely sticks; training-heavy tools struggled | NITI 150; AIC 38, 52 |
| *Opinion:* 4-day induction; seniors brush newcomers off ("ye sab aise hi chalta hai") | APU 2, 8 |
| *Opinion:* DIET faculty rarely in schools; support is "inspection and formality"; mentors chosen without criteria; cascade loss | APU 4, 7, 11; IDR 2 |

**Responses:**
- Sakhee AI coach: 74% of 50 teachers improved, 87% found feedback helpful (AIC 74, 75).
- ShikshaSaathi: 7,238 Anganwadi workers, NPS 43.6 (AIC 72, 73).
- Coaching models: CM Rise, LLF, Funda Wande, Tusome, Room to Read, CARE, Pratham, STIR, Rocket Learning (FLN 60, 61, 67, 89, 127, 130, 132–134).
- Follow-up: Goa, Indra AI, Bookbot (AIC 28, 29, 39, 51); Gyankunj, Sunehra Kal (NITI 172, 220); Mission Prerna (FLN 46, 66); online training (IDR 2).
- Japan: about 300 mentor hours in year one (APU 9).
- Refreshers: up to 10 days (FLN 97).
- Proposed: mentoring-led supervision, coach training, yearlong induction (NITI 177, 178; FLN 139, 142–144; APU 10).

## G2. Training is theoretical: little practice, nothing to watch, no feedback

| Evidence | Sources |
|---|---|
| 75% say training rarely demonstrates or practises (one State); 36% recall child-centred pedagogy; a third found it useful | FLN 56; NITI 147 |
| Tech training topics: digital literacy 29%, safe use 20%, tools 19%; integration and AI 9% each, policy 12%, State programmes 7% (?) | BaSE 60 |
| Low-use teachers least trained: tools 8% vs 38%; integration 3% vs 22% | BaSE 77 |
| 32% of non-users lack know-how; half of the 46% claiming GenAI understanding equate it with search | BaSE 56, 66 |
| Induction: 3.5 of 22 hours on subject pedagogy | APU 8 |
| Pre-service practice: 5–6 weeks, poorly supervised, generic feedback | FLN 107, 109; NITI 146, 147 |
| No State checks what teachers learned; NISHTHA can't test understanding | FLN 56; IDR 4 |
| Trainers lecture even on activity-based learning | FLN 55, 56 |
| Training skips multigrade, mixed levels, differentiation, assessment design; AI training generic | NITI 139, 150; FLN 53 |
| Observation feels evaluative; learning by watching AI is informal | AIC 42, 74, 75 |
| *Opinion:* BRC workshops (6–10 days a year) lack a curriculum; weak mentors limit exposure to good teaching (?) | APU 6, 10 |

Video exemplars of good teaching: Not found. Live demonstration appears only in CSF's training design and Funda Wande coaching (FLN 62, 67).

**Responses:**
- Structured pedagogy with practical training has the largest consistent effect: 0.14 SD maths, 0.23 SD literacy (FLN 64).
- Sakhee lifted key teaching steps from 53% to 74% (AIC 74, 75).
- Goa's live-course programme cut prep about 20% (AIC 38, 39).
- TOY8 certified 157 teachers (AIC 85); Chimple reported gains (BaSE 17, 60).
- Proposed: Model Practice Schools, observation hours, feedback formats, peer and AI-assisted observation (NITI 177, 186, 187); mentors observing new teachers (APU 11).

## G3. One-size-fits-all training

| Evidence | Sources |
|---|---|
| 3 of 4 States skip needs analysis; one used NAS data | FLN 54 |
| Three teacher segments (48/18/34%); older teachers see less impact (69% vs 83%), save less time (44%); non-use reasons vary by age (?) | BaSE 61, 76, 77, 86 |
| Training access: govt 55% vs private 41%; under-30 43% vs 51–60 59% | BaSE 60 |
| 63% of schools are multigrade, yet teachers spend 55% of time on one grade | FLN 35 |
| Over 70% of Goa faculty use AI informally; under 52% integrate it | AIC 38 |
| 79% want more training, uniformly across groups | BaSE 61 |
| No in-service policy; courses pre-designed, cascade-diluted, silent on multilingual teaching | FLN 36, 54, 55, 57 |
| Generic capacity-building and B.Ed.; no subject-wise post data to target support | NITI 147, 150 |
| *Opinion:* Training fatigue: too many mandatory, irrelevant programmes (1 lakh+ teachers, 4 States, 36 districts) | IDR 3 |
| *Opinion:* Novices and near-retirees get the same programme; "teacher deficiency" assumption; same teachers sent yearly (anecdotal) | IDR 3 |

Training designed around teacher needs or experience: Not found (BaSE, FLN, AIC, NITI).

**Responses:**
- Goa: six discipline clusters, two master trainers each (AIC 38, 39).
- Sakhee targets each teacher's priority practices (AIC 74).
- Delhi designs training bottom-up (FLN 102); Odisha ran 15-day multilingual training (FLN 40).
- Singapore, South Korea and Australia let teachers choose (IDR 4).
- Policy already calls for need-based development (APU 3, 4).
- Proposed: subject diagnostics and career-staged CPD (NITI 186, 187), or self-directed credits (IDR 5).

## G4. Institutions can't see what reaches teachers or classrooms

| Evidence | Sources |
|---|---|
| 80% of monitoring indicators track inputs; 50% of BRC and 48% of CRC formats omit scores; 62% of CRCs, 49% of heads see monitoring as data-gathering | FLN 72, 73 |
| Paper assessment data inflated ~20% (Andhra Pradesh) | FLN 75, 80 |
| Adoption: 41% of teachers seen using textbooks; other TLM in 10% of classrooms; 54% have read NCF-SE | FLN 49; NITI 150 |
| Self-reported reach: 51% had tech training last year; DIKSHA 21% (govt 26%, private 7%, aged 51–60 45%); NISHTHA 13% (17%, 3%, 31%); 27% started via govt campaign | BaSE 57, 58, 60, 84 |
| Access exceeds use: smart class 44% vs 40%; laptops 34% vs 24%; tablets 29% vs 21%; govt devices 22% vs 18% | BaSE 52, 53 |
| 56% use EdTech daily; 77% use it for communication, 63% for lessons; 51% use GenAI; 9% of trained teachers covered AI (?) | BaSE 56, 58, 60, 66 |
| Systems count teachers trained, not competencies or classroom use | FLN 54; IDR 4 |
| Visit data unused; supervision chases visible indicators; monitoring backward-looking | FLN 74; NITI 145, 177; AIC 86 |
| No shared grade-3 goals across stakeholders (4 States) | FLN 44 |
| Little systematic adoption data; first teacher survey (2,500, 10 States) | BaSE 17, 18, 22 |
| *Opinion:* NISHTHA can't verify who completed courses; budgets need no effectiveness evidence; training is compliance | IDR 3, 4 |

**Responses:**
- DFD flags raised Karnataka school visits to 78% (AIC 86, 87).
- Usage data: DIKSHA 2.98B plays, 87% from teacher courses (FLN 68); Aural over 70% weekly teacher use; Khanmigo 20,000–25,000 weekly teachers (AIC 25, 52).
- Dashboards: PadhaiWithAI, Indra AI, Rocket Learning, Goa, Babson (AIC 23, 39, 46, 47, 51, 73); CM Rise, TNVN, Tangerine (FLN 60, 78, 79, 148); UP GLP app, SATH-E inspections (NITI 166, 201).
- No adoption data for NISHTHA, DIKSHA or NIPUN (NITI 167, 200, 202, 203, 209; AIC 74).
- Proposed: visit logs, deployment dashboards, time-on-task and CPD tracking (NITI 178, 185, 187, 189, 222); DIKSHA portfolios (IDR 5).

## Where the documents disagree

| Issue | One view | Other view |
|---|---|---|
| Is training valued? | A third find it useful (FLN 56) | 62% very satisfied (BaSE 60) |
| Too little or too much? | Under a third trained yearly (FLN 54); 4-day induction (APU 8) | 51% trained last year (BaSE 60); 2 lakh (Karnataka), 2.3 lakh (UP) trained (NITI 187, 201); 7–8 days a year (FLN 27); training fatigue (IDR 3) |
| Does training change practice? | Practical training and feedback work (FLN 64; AIC 39, 75) | No link to scores (FLN 136, 140); untrained enthusiasts adopt (BaSE 76, 77); adoption flat across groups (BaSE 55, 56); little training needed (AIC 53); STIR mixed (FLN 127) |
| Main barrier | Training quality (FLN 54–56; NITI 147) | Access: 47% vs knowledge 32% among non-users (BaSE 56); 68% school-device problems (BaSE 53) |
| Fixing one-size-fits-all | Diagnose needs, tier CPD (NITI 186, 187); discipline clusters (AIC 39) | Uniform State modules (NITI 186); at scale, credits instead (IDR 5), though devaluable (IDR 6) |
| Can institutions see? | Counts and compliance only (FLN 54, 73; IDR 3, 4) | Dashboards exist (AIC 46, 47, 86, 87; FLN 60, 68, 78, 148; NITI 166, 201); supervision shifting (NITI 177) |
| NISHTHA | "Promising step" (IDR 3) | Completion unverifiable (IDR 4); used by 13% (BaSE 58) |

**Why they may differ.**
- **BaSE** measures self-reported technology training among EdTech users only (N=2,186), at most two core teachers per school, 67% government. Figures vary slightly across sections and the annex has typos (BaSE 12, 23, 28, 55, 60, 66, 83, 84, 86).
- **FLN** is an older five-State diagnostic of government primary schools (FLN 6, 8).
- **AIC** showcases 36 of 271 submissions with small pilots (Sakhee 50 teachers, Chimple 5, CrazyGoldfish 12), some in higher education or Class 10. Only 68% of Sakhee teachers were comfortable recording (AIC 11, 13, 23, 31, 33, 38, 46, 75).
- **NITI** is not a policy statement and has no teacher survey (NITI 2, 36, 37).
- **IDR and APU** are opinion pieces; neither shows that international models transfer to India (IDR 3, 4; APU 1, 9).
